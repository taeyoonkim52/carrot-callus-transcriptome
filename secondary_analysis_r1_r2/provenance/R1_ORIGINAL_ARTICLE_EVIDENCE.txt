Data Description
3 Data Description This study selected four carrot materials with varying callus induction and differentiation capacities for comparative transcriptomic analysis, including three cultivated carrot ( Daucus carota  var.  sativus ) accessions (C815, C819, and C824) and one wild carrot ( Daucus carota  subsp.  carota ) accession (Ws). The callus induction rates of the four materials all reached 100%, while their callus differentiation rates were 25%, 12.5%, 2% and 0%, respectively. RNA samples were extracted at 0, 20, 70, and 120 days after hypocotyl culture. resulting in a total of 48 sequencing libraries. After library preparation, paired-end sequencing was performed using the MGI platform .This study obtained a total of 2,663,222,674 clean reads and 399.48 Gb of clean bases, with an average of 6 Gb per library and a Q30 base percentage of 97% or higher. In the obtained transcriptome samples, the total reads range between 3 million and 7 million overall, with reads mapped generally exceeding 85%, unique mapped falling between 60% and 80%, and multi mapped below 20%. These result demonstrates that the sequencing data are of high quality and the alignment to the reference genome is efficient. The raw sequence read data are deposited in the NCBI BioProject Sequence Read Archive (SRA) database under accession number PRJNA1398431. Summaries of the RNA Integrity Number (RIN), as well as raw and filtered read data are presented in  Table 1 . Summaries of reference genome alignment results are presented in  Table 2 . Aggregate reports of raw read quality from FastP followed by MultiQC analysis are presented in  Fig. 2 . Principal component analysis (PCA) of all samples is presented in  Fig. 3 . Volcano plots comparing transcript expression levels in cultured tissues between the wild genotype Ws and the cultivated genotype C815 at the same time point, as well as transcript expression levels in C815 cultured tissues at different time points, are presented in  Fig. 4 . Sample collection
4.1 Sample collection Four carrot accessions with different differentiation ability were sourced from the Institute of Vegetables and Flowers, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences, including three local cultivated carrot (C815, C819, and C824) and one wild carrot Ws. The sterilized seeds of the four materials were inoculated on MS basal medium for 10 days to obtain sterile seedlings. Hypocotyls were excised for subsequent culture and RNA sample extraction. For callus induction (0–20 days after culture), hypocotyl explants from the four materials were cultured on MS basal medium supplemented with 1 mg/L 2,4-dichlorophenoxyacetic acid (2,4-D) and 1 mg/L 6-benzyladenine (6-BA). For shoot regeneration (20–70 days), induced calli were transferred to hormone-free MS basal medium. All cultures were maintained at 26°C under a 16 h light / 8 h dark photoperiod. Tissue samples were collected at 0, 20, 70, and 120 days after hypocotyl culture initiation. At each time point, three entire cultured tissues from a single Petri dish as one biological replicate, including callus and early differentiated structures, and three independent Petri dishes were used to generate three biological replicates. All samples were ground to a fine powder in liquid nitrogen and stored at –80°C for subsequent RNA extraction. Total RNA was isolated using Tri-RNA Reagent. To ensure high-quality input for transcriptome sequencing, RNA concentration was measured with a Qubit 4.0 Fluorometer/MD microplate reader, and integrity was precisely detected using a Qsep400 Bioanalyzer [ 4 ].
